2. The four-field rule

Every published row carries all four, or it is not published as a fact:

#fieldwhere it comes from
1case1RC191003776 or CAAP-26-0000625 — no row is case-less
2docketthe docket number, plus the register page or the citation
3evidencea published file, a SHA-256 of the held file, or an explicit GAP
4gradeOBS / PARTIAL / GAP, with for counsel wherever a legal conclusion would be needed

The "for counsel" boundary. The site states what the documents say and where they say it. It draws no legal conclusion, gives no advice, and computes no deadline: on /appeal the two dates stated in ICA Dkt 17 p.1 are printed as verbatim quotations and marked for counsel, because whether they bind and what satisfies them are legal questions. Where a fact would need a legal judgement, the row says for counsel instead of answering.

Tiers: what "best available" means

A file's tier states what was obtainable at capture time, not how good the copy is. Certified means the court's certified download was available and taken; best available means the certified channel was already consumed, so the current regular copy was taken now, hashed, and compared with the file already held — on 2026-11-03, nine of nine were byte-identical. "Best available" is therefore a statement about obtainability, not quality, and it is never rendered as "certified". If a later capture of the same docket returns a different hash, the earlier file is not overwritten: it is kept and recorded as a superseded rendition next to the new one, each with its own hash, capture time and telemetry record.

4. What is NOT published, and why

No PDF for 454 of the 495 trial dockets (495 − 41 that hold a PDF of their own document). 491 of 495 carry a text rendition; those rows are published on /register, and the text itself is published on /texts. No PDF exists to publish.

5 appeal docket rows are GAP (dockets 12, 14, 16, 18, 20) — never captured. They are published as explicit GAP rows on /appeal rather than guessed.

Some certificates are courthouse-only — no certified copy of Dkt 480, and no certified copies of the Dkt 486/487 minutes, is in the export; the minutes' text is.

Nothing on this site is redacted or altered, and no row silently disappears: a row with no published file says so and names the file and hash that is held.

Which export is authoritative

The 70-document export is the authoritative one (50,453,826 bytes; MANIFEST.csv 15,625 B 954cd1c1488431b9…, MANIFEST.sha256 14,013 B 7e12153e59c77ba4…, README 0d0bf3ca82c484e7…).

The earlier 37-document set is superseded and archived, and the 70-document set is superseded by the current 80-document export (19,147,433 bytes; MANIFEST.csv 7,079 B a0c7c8e9…, MANIFEST.sha256 6,272 B caac1843…). Nothing on this site is derived from it any more: every hash and size above is computed from the files served in the same build, and the build fails if a published claim does not equal the bytes served.

The trial snapshot reconciles the record end-to-end: the court’s page renders 503 docket rows, and this site’s register is 495 rows plus the 8-docket tail (496–503) — 495 + 8 = 503. See /snapshots#reconciliation.
